## Intended use

- Short-form English instruction following, in the style of the training corpus.
- Demonstrating and benchmarking parameter-efficient fine-tuning trade-offs.

## Out of scope

- **Factual lookup.** A model this size hallucinates confidently.
- **Medical, legal or financial advice.**
- **Any decision affecting a person** — hiring, credit, moderation, safety.
- **Languages other than English**, which the training data barely covers.

## Limitations

- **The quality metrics measure n-gram overlap with a single reference answer.**
  A correct paraphrase scores badly; a fluent falsehood scores well. Read
  `evaluation/samples.md` in the run artifacts before trusting the aggregate.
- **200 evaluation examples** is enough to rank candidates against each other and
  too few to claim an absolute capability.
- **Latency and memory are hardware-specific**, measured on the environment
  recorded in the run's `env.*` tags. Different hardware, different numbers.
- **Cost is a derived estimate** from measured throughput and a stated hourly
  rate. It ignores cold starts, idle time and autoscaling.
- **Inherited bias.** Fine-tuning does not remove what the base model learned
  during pretraining.
- **Single seed.** Run-to-run variance is not characterised, so small differences
  between arms may be noise. Training each arm twice would fix this and is listed
  under future work for a reason.

## Why this section exists

The limitations section is the one most people skip, and it is the one that makes
the rest credible. A card that lists only strengths tells a reader that nobody
looked hard at the model. Anyone can report a good number; being precise about
what the number does not cover is the part that takes judgement.
